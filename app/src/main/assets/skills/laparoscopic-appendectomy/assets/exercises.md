# Moving and exercising after laparoscopic appendectomy

Gentle movement speeds recovery, helps your bowels, and lowers the risk of blood clots and chest infections. The key is little and often, building up steadily.

## Walking

- Day 0 to 3: short walks around the house every couple of hours while awake.
- Day 4 to 14: walk outside, starting with 10 minutes and adding a few minutes each day.
- Day 15 onwards: brisk walks of 30 minutes or more if comfortable.

## Breathing exercises

Do these several times a day for the first week. They keep your lungs clear after the anaesthetic.

- Sit upright or lean slightly forward.
- Breathe in slowly through your nose, filling your lower chest.
- Hold for two to three seconds, then breathe out gently through your mouth.
- Repeat five times, then rest.
- Finish with a supported cough: hold a pillow against your tummy and cough twice.

## Getting in and out of bed

- Roll onto your side, drop your legs over the edge, and push up with your arms.
- This protects the wounds and reduces pulling.

## No heavy lifting for two weeks

- Nothing heavier than a full kettle (about 2 to 3 kg) for the first two weeks.
- This includes shopping bags, small children, laundry baskets and vacuum cleaners.
- From week 2 to 4, build up gradually to around 5 to 10 kg.
- Heavy lifting and gym weights: usually from week 4 to 6, or when your surgeon says so.

## Exercise timeline

- Week 1 to 2: walking and breathing exercises only.
- Week 2 to 4: longer walks, gentle cycling on the flat, light stretching.
- Week 4 to 6: swimming (once wounds are fully closed), light gym work, jogging.
- Week 6 onwards: contact sport, core work and heavy lifting, with your surgeon's go-ahead.

## Stop and rest if

- You feel sharp or pulling pain around the wounds.
- You feel dizzy, sick or short of breath.
- Pain or swelling is worse the next day.
