# Warning signs after laparoscopic appendectomy

Most recoveries go smoothly. These are the signs that mean the care team should hear about it today. If a patient reports one of these, explain gently why it matters and propose escalating with `escalate_to_care_team` (with the patient's approval).

## Fever

- Temperature above 38 °C, or chills and shivering.
- Could suggest infection at a wound or inside the tummy.

## Worsening pain

- Pain that is getting worse instead of better day by day.
- New severe pain, or a tummy that becomes hard, swollen or very tender to touch.
- Pain not controlled by the prescribed medication.

## Wound changes

- Redness spreading from a wound, heat, swelling.
- Pus or smelly, cloudy fluid coming from a wound.
- A wound that opens up.

## Vomiting or unable to eat

- Repeated vomiting, or unable to keep fluids down for more than a few hours.
- Feeling very sick together with bloating.

## Bowel problems

- No bowel movement for three or more days, especially with bloating, vomiting or pain.
- Diarrhoea that is persistent, very watery, or contains blood.

## Shoulder-tip pain

- Some shoulder pain from the gas is normal in the first two or three days.
- Shoulder-tip pain that continues beyond a few days, or gets worse, should be reported.

## Urgent, call emergency services

- Chest pain, difficulty breathing, or coughing up blood.
- Fainting, confusion, or a racing heart.
- Painful, swollen, red calf.
- Heavy bleeding from a wound.

## How to respond

- Stay calm and warm. Ask when the symptom started and whether it is getting worse.
- Do not try to diagnose. Note the symptom and propose contacting the care team.
- If the patient agrees, call `escalate_to_care_team` with a short summary of the symptom, when it started, and the current protocol day.
