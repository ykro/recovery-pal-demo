# Phase 2 — Days 4 to 14: Getting back to your routine

By now the worst is usually behind you. This phase is about building up your daily activity while still protecting the wounds inside and out.

## What is normal

- Energy coming back gradually, with the odd tired afternoon.
- Wounds starting to itch as they heal, with small scabs forming.
- Bruising turning yellow and fading.
- Mild twinges in your tummy when you twist or stand up quickly.
- Bowels settling into a more regular pattern.

## Activity allowed

- Longer walks outside, building up a little each day.
- Light household tasks such as cooking and washing up.
- Most people can return to a desk job or study around the end of this phase, if they feel ready.
- Driving is usually fine once you can do an emergency stop without pain and are no longer taking strong painkillers. Check with your insurer.
- Still no heavy lifting (nothing above about 5 kg), no contact sport, no gym.

## Pain expectations

- Pain should be mild and improving. Many people stop regular painkillers during this phase.
- Occasional aching after a busy day is normal.
- Shoulder-tip pain should be gone by now.

## What to check daily

- Temperature if you feel unwell or shivery.
- Wounds: dressings can usually come off now. Look for redness, swelling or fluid.
- Bowel movements: regular and not painful.
- Appetite and fluids back to normal.
- Sleep improving.

## When to worry

- Temperature above 38 °C.
- Pain that is getting worse, or new pain that does not settle with rest.
- Redness spreading from a wound, pus, or a wound that opens up.
- Vomiting or being unable to eat for more than a day.
- No bowel movement for several days with bloating or pain.
- Shoulder-tip pain that continues beyond the first few days.
