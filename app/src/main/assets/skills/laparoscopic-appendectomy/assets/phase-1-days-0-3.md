# Phase 1 — Days 0 to 3: The first few days home

You have just had keyhole surgery. Your body is doing a lot of healing right now, so the main job this week is to rest, move gently, and keep an eye on things.

## What is normal

- Feeling tired and wanting to sleep more than usual.
- Soreness around the three small wounds on your tummy.
- Bloating and a bit of trapped wind from the gas used during surgery.
- Pain in the tip of one or both shoulders (also from the gas). This usually fades within a few days.
- Mild bruising around the wounds.
- A sore throat if you had a breathing tube during the anaesthetic.

## Activity allowed

- Short, gentle walks around the house several times a day. This helps your bowels and lowers the risk of blood clots.
- Climbing stairs slowly is fine.
- Rest whenever you feel tired. Do not push through it.
- No driving, no lifting anything heavier than a kettle, no sport.

## Pain expectations

- Pain is usually moderate on day 0 and 1 and eases every day.
- Take your pain relief regularly as prescribed, rather than waiting for the pain to build.
- Hold a pillow against your tummy when you cough, laugh or sneeze.

## What to check daily

- Temperature, once in the morning and once in the evening.
- The three wounds: dressings dry, no spreading redness, no fluid leaking.
- Are you passing wind? Have you had a bowel movement yet?
- Are you drinking enough and managing small meals?
- Is pain getting a little better each day?

## When to worry

- Temperature above 38 °C.
- Pain that is getting worse rather than better, or new severe pain.
- Vomiting, or unable to keep fluids down.
- A wound that is red, hot, swollen or leaking pus.
- Tummy that becomes hard, very swollen or very tender.
- No bowel movement by day 3 together with bloating or vomiting.

If any of these happen, tell me and we can contact your care team together.
