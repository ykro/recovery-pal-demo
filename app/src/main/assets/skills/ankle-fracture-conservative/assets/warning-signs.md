# Warning signs with an ankle fracture in a cast or boot

Most people heal without trouble. These signs need attention today. If a patient reports one, explain gently why it matters and propose escalating with `escalate_to_care_team` (with the patient's approval). Chest symptoms are an emergency.

## Numbness or tingling

- Pins and needles, numbness, or loss of feeling in the toes or foot.
- Unable to wiggle the toes.
- May mean pressure on a nerve or the cast is too tight.

## Blue, white or cold toes

- Toes that look pale, blue, purple or dusky, or feel cold compared with the other foot.
- Toes that stay white for more than a couple of seconds after pressing on the nail.
- May mean circulation is being squeezed.

## Cast too tight

- Increasing pain that does not ease with elevation and painkillers.
- A feeling of tightness, throbbing or pressure that is getting worse.
- Swelling above the cast edge, or the cast cutting into the skin.
- Burning or rubbing pain at one particular spot.
- These can signal dangerous pressure inside the cast. Raise the leg and contact the team straight away.

## Calf pain or swelling (possible blood clot)

- Pain, tenderness, warmth or swelling in the calf of either leg.
- Redness or a firm, cord-like feeling in the calf.
- Immobility raises the risk of deep vein thrombosis. This needs same-day assessment.

## Shortness of breath or chest pain

- Sudden breathlessness, chest pain, coughing up blood, racing heart or fainting.
- Could be a clot that has travelled to the lungs. Call emergency services immediately.

## Cast or boot problems

- Cracked, soft, wet or loose cast.
- Something dropped inside.
- Bad smell, or a discharge or stain coming through.
- Open skin or sores at the edges.

## How to respond

- Stay calm and warm. Ask when it started and whether it is getting worse.
- For toe colour, numbness or tightness: advise elevating the leg now and contacting the team.
- For chest symptoms: advise calling emergency services first.
- If the patient agrees, call `escalate_to_care_team` with a short summary of the symptom, when it started, and the current protocol day.
