# Phase 2 — Days 22 to 42: Bone knitting, getting stronger

The bone is now forming a solid bridge. Your team may swap the cast for a boot or allow partial weight bearing after an X-ray. Follow their instructions exactly, because every fracture heals at its own pace.

## What is normal

- Much less pain. Many people are off regular painkillers.
- Swelling that comes and goes, especially after time on your feet.
- Itching and dry, flaky skin under the cast or boot.
- Muscle wasting in the calf and thigh. The leg looks thinner. This recovers with rehab.
- Stiffness in the knee and hip from limited movement.

## Activity allowed

- Continue non-weight bearing unless your team has cleared you. If they have, put through only the amount of weight they specified, always with crutches.
- If you have a removable boot, you may have been told to take it off for washing or gentle movement. Only do this if told.
- Longer outings on crutches. Pace yourself and elevate afterwards.
- Return to a desk job is often possible now if you can travel safely and elevate at work.
- No driving until you are out of the cast or boot and cleared by your team.

## Pain expectations

- Mild ache after activity, settling with rest and elevation.
- Sharp pain when starting to weight bear is a signal to back off and report.

## What to check daily

- Toes: colour, warmth, feeling, movement.
- Swelling: settling overnight.
- Cast or boot: fit, edges, no pressure points.
- Skin around the edges: no sores or redness.
- Calves: no new tenderness or swelling.
- Do your toe, knee and hip exercises.

## When to worry

- Any change in toe colour, warmth or feeling.
- Increasing pain, or pain at a single point under the cast.
- Calf pain or swelling, especially one-sided.
- Shortness of breath or chest pain, which is an emergency.
- Cast damage, or a boot that no longer fits because of swelling changes.
- Sores or open skin at the cast edges.
