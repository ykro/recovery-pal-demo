# Phase 3 — Days 43 onwards: Cast off and rebuilding

Most conservatively treated ankle fractures come out of the cast or boot around six weeks. Now the work shifts to regaining movement, strength and confidence.

## What is normal

- A stiff, weak ankle that does not bend or point fully. This is expected after weeks of immobility.
- Skin that is dry, flaky and sensitive. Gentle washing and moisturiser help.
- Swelling by the end of the day for several weeks or months, easing with elevation.
- Calf thinner than the other side.
- A limp for the first few weeks of walking.
- Aching after exercise or a long day.

## Activity allowed

- Weight bearing as instructed by your team, usually progressing from partial to full over a couple of weeks.
- Physiotherapy exercises daily. Start with range of motion, then strength, then balance.
- Walking on flat ground, gradually increasing distance.
- Swimming and stationary cycling are usually fine once the skin is settled.
- Driving once you can perform an emergency stop confidently, usually two to four weeks after cast removal.
- Running, jumping and sport: usually from three months, with your team's go-ahead.

## Pain expectations

- Mild aching and stiffness that improves as the day goes on.
- Sharper pain during exercise means ease off, not stop entirely.
- Pain should trend downwards week by week.

## What to check daily

- Ankle movement: a little more each week.
- Swelling: settles overnight.
- Skin: no sores, healing well.
- Walking pattern: limp reducing.
- Calves: no new tenderness.

## When to worry

- Sudden sharp pain in the ankle, or a new inability to bear weight.
- Swelling that gets worse day after day rather than better.
- Calf pain or swelling, or shortness of breath, which is an emergency.
- Numbness or tingling in the foot.
- Ankle giving way repeatedly.
- No improvement in movement after several weeks of exercises.
