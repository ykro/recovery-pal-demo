# Phase 1 — Days 0 to 21: Protecting the ankle

The bone has started to knit but is fragile. Your cast or boot holds everything still. This phase is about keeping weight off, keeping the swelling down, and keeping the cast in good shape.

## What is normal

- Swelling of the foot and toes, worst in the first week and after standing.
- Bruising around the ankle, foot and sometimes up the shin, changing colour as it fades.
- A dull ache, especially at night and after being upright.
- Itching under the cast.
- Feeling clumsy and tired from getting around on crutches.

## Activity allowed

- Non-weight bearing on the injured leg unless your team has told you otherwise. Use crutches or a frame for every step.
- Rest with the leg raised above the level of your heart as much as possible, especially the first week.
- Short trips on crutches for essentials. Sit down for tasks.
- Keep the cast dry. Use a waterproof cover or a plastic bag sealed above the cast for showers.
- No driving.

## Pain expectations

- Moderate ache in the first few days, easing steadily.
- Take painkillers regularly as prescribed. Elevation is your best painkiller.
- Pain should not be sharp, burning, or increasing under the cast.

## What to check daily

- Toes: pink, warm, able to wiggle, normal feeling when touched.
- Swelling: less when elevated, not getting worse day after day.
- Cast: intact, no cracks, no soft or wet patches, no rubbing at the edges.
- Calves: no tenderness or swelling in either leg.
- Move your toes, knee and hip several times a day.

## When to worry

- Toes that turn blue, white or feel cold.
- Numbness, pins and needles, or you cannot move your toes.
- Pain that increases and does not ease with elevation, or a cast that feels too tight.
- Burning or rubbing pain at one spot under the cast.
- Calf pain, tenderness or swelling in either leg.
- Shortness of breath or chest pain, which is an emergency.
- The cast cracks, gets wet or loosens.
