# Looking after your cast or boot

Your cast or boot only works if it stays intact, dry and comfortable. A few simple habits keep it that way.

## Keep it dry

- A wet plaster cast softens and stops holding the bone. A wet fibreglass cast can cause skin problems.
- For showers, use a purpose-made waterproof cover or a strong plastic bag sealed with tape above the cast. Keep the leg out of the water flow.
- If the cast gets wet, dry it with a towel and a hair dryer on cool. If it stays soft or smells, contact your team.
- Removable boots: take off and dry the liner if it gets wet, only if you have been told you may remove it.

## Elevation

- Raise the leg on pillows so the ankle is above your heart, especially in the first two weeks and after being upright.
- Elevation reduces swelling, pain and pressure inside the cast.

## Itching

- Do not push anything down the cast (pens, knitting needles, coat hangers). Scratches under a cast can become infected.
- Try tapping the cast, a cool hair dryer down the top, or an antihistamine if your team agrees.

## Keep it clean and intact

- Do not trim, cut or pad the edges yourself.
- Do not get sand, dirt or lotions inside.
- Check the edges daily for rubbing, redness or sores.
- Do not walk on a cast unless it is a walking cast and your team said so.

## Boot fit

- Loosen the straps slightly if the foot swells during the day, then re-tighten when it settles.
- Wear a clean sock or liner every day.
- Check the heel and shin for pressure marks.

## Crutches and getting around

- Keep the cast on the floor only for balance if told partial weight bearing, otherwise hold it up.
- Remove rugs and clutter at home.
- Sit to shower, dress and cook.

## Contact your team if

- The cast cracks, loosens, or is too tight.
- Something is stuck inside.
- The cast smells bad, or there is a wet patch or stain.
- Skin at the edges is sore or broken.
