# Phase 3 — Days 43 onwards: Walking again

Around six weeks your team usually confirms on X-ray that the bone is healing well and clears you for full weight bearing, often out of the boot. Now the work is rehabilitation: movement, strength, balance and confidence.

## What is normal

- A stiff, weak ankle that does not move fully at first.
- Swelling by the end of the day for several weeks or months.
- A firm, sometimes numb scar. Gentle massage once fully healed helps it soften.
- Being aware of the plate under the skin, especially in cold weather or when kneeling. This is common and usually settles.
- A limp for the first few weeks.
- Aching after exercise or a long day.

## Activity allowed

- Full weight bearing as instructed, progressing from crutches to one crutch to none.
- Daily physiotherapy exercises: range of motion first, then strength and balance.
- Walking on flat ground, building distance gradually.
- Swimming and stationary cycling once the wound is fully healed and your team agrees.
- Driving once you can do an emergency stop confidently, typically a few weeks after full weight bearing.
- Running, jumping and sport: usually from three to four months, with your team's go-ahead.

## Pain expectations

- Mild aching and morning stiffness that eases with movement.
- Sharper pain during exercise means ease off.
- Pain should trend downwards week by week.

## What to check daily

- Ankle movement improving.
- Swelling settling overnight.
- Scar healthy, no new redness or fluid.
- Walking pattern improving.
- Calves: no new tenderness.

## When to worry

- New or increasing pain over the metalwork, or pain that stops you walking.
- Scar becomes red, warm, or leaks fluid at any point, even months later.
- Temperature above 38 °C.
- Calf pain or swelling, or chest pain or breathlessness, which is an emergency.
- Ankle giving way repeatedly, or a sudden loss of ability to bear weight.
- No progress in movement after several weeks of exercises.
