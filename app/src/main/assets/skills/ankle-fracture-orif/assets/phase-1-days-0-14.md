# Phase 1 — Days 0 to 14: Healing the wound

Your ankle has been fixed with a plate and screws, and the incision needs these two weeks to close. Your leg is in a backslab, cast or boot. This phase is all about elevation, protecting the wound, and keeping weight off.

## What is normal

- Swelling of the foot and toes, worst in the first week.
- Bruising around the ankle and foot, sometimes tracking up the shin.
- Aching and throbbing, worse at night and whenever the leg hangs down.
- Numbness or tingling in a small patch of skin near the incision.
- Tiredness for several days after the anaesthetic.
- A small amount of dried blood on the dressing from the first day or two.

## Activity allowed

- Non-weight bearing on the operated leg. Use crutches or a frame for every step.
- Elevate the leg above heart level as much as possible, aim for most of the day.
- Short trips for essentials only. Sit to wash, dress and cook.
- Keep the cast and dressing completely dry.
- Take any blood-thinning medication exactly as prescribed.
- No driving.

## Pain expectations

- Moderate to significant pain in the first three to five days, easing after that.
- Take painkillers regularly as prescribed, do not wait for the pain to build.
- Elevation is the most effective way to lower pain.

## What to check daily

- Toes: pink, warm, able to wiggle, normal feeling.
- Dressing: dry, no fresh soaking, no smell.
- Swelling: less after elevation.
- Temperature once a day, or whenever you feel shivery.
- Calves: no tenderness or swelling in either leg.
- Toe, knee and hip movements done.

## When to worry

- Fresh blood or fluid soaking through the dressing, or any smelly discharge.
- Temperature above 38 °C, or chills.
- Toes that turn blue, white or cold, or you lose feeling in them.
- Pain that increases and does not ease with elevation and painkillers.
- Calf pain, swelling or tenderness.
- Chest pain or shortness of breath, which is an emergency.
