# Wound care after ankle ORIF

You have one or two incisions over the ankle, closed with sutures or staples. Ankle skin is thin and heals slowly, so protecting the wound in the first two weeks matters a great deal.

## Dressings

- Leave the dressing and cast or backslab alone unless your team has told you to change it.
- If fluid or blood strikes through, do not remove the dressing. Add a clean pad over the top and contact your team.
- Do not apply creams, lotions or powders.

## Keep it dry

- No water on the wound or cast for the first two weeks.
- Use a waterproof cover or sealed plastic bag for showers, and keep the leg out of the water flow.
- Once the wound is fully closed and your team agrees, water running over it is fine. Pat dry, do not rub.
- No baths, hot tubs or swimming until the wound has been closed for at least a week.

## Sutures or staples removal

- These are usually removed at around two weeks, at the clinic or by a nurse.
- Removal is quick and mildly uncomfortable rather than painful.
- Small paper strips may be applied afterwards. Let them fall off on their own.
- After removal the scar is still delicate, avoid stretching or rubbing it for another week.

## What healthy healing looks like

- Pink or slightly red edges that fade over weeks.
- Mild itching.
- A small area of numbness next to the scar.
- Slight puckering or a firm ridge, which softens over months.

## Scar care (after full healing)

- Once no scabs remain, moisturise daily with a plain unscented cream.
- Gentle circular massage for a few minutes a day helps the scar soften.
- Protect the scar from sun for the first year with clothing or high-factor sunscreen.

## Contact your team if

- The wound opens, gapes, or you can see anything underneath.
- Redness spreading, warmth, or increasing swelling around the scar.
- Cloudy, yellow, green or smelly discharge.
- Fever alongside any wound change.
- Any part of the metalwork visible or felt through the skin.
