# Phase 2 — Days 15 to 42: Wound closed, bone knitting

The incision is closed and the sutures or staples are usually out. The bone is now healing around the metalwork. Your team may have moved you into a removable boot and given specific weight-bearing instructions. Follow those exactly.

## What is normal

- A pink, slightly raised scar that is tender to touch.
- Numbness near the scar that may last months.
- Swelling that returns after time on your feet and settles overnight.
- Muscle wasting in the calf. The leg looks thinner. This recovers.
- Stiffness in the ankle if you have been allowed to remove the boot and move it.
- Occasional sharp twinges as nerves recover.

## Activity allowed

- Weight bearing only as your team has specified: non-weight bearing, touch, partial or full. Always with crutches until told otherwise.
- If the boot is removable, you may have been told to take it off for washing and gentle ankle movement. Only do this if instructed.
- Longer outings on crutches. Elevate afterwards.
- Return to a desk job is often possible if you can travel safely and elevate at work.
- Showering without a cover once the wound is fully closed and your team agrees.
- No driving until out of the boot and cleared.

## Pain expectations

- Mild ache, worse after activity, settling with rest and elevation.
- Sharp pain over the metalwork when weight bearing is a signal to back off and report.
- Most people are off strong painkillers by now.

## What to check daily

- Scar: closed, dry, no new redness or fluid.
- Toes: colour, warmth, feeling.
- Swelling: settling overnight.
- Boot fit and skin at the edges.
- Calves: no new tenderness or swelling.
- Exercises done as prescribed.

## When to worry

- Wound opening, oozing, or a new area of redness or warmth around the scar.
- Temperature above 38 °C.
- New sharp pain over the plate or screws, especially with weight bearing.
- Calf pain or swelling.
- Chest pain or shortness of breath, which is an emergency.
- Any hardware you can feel or see poking under or through the skin.
