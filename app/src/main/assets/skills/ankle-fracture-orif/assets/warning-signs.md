# Warning signs after ankle ORIF

Most people recover without trouble. These signs need same-day attention. If a patient reports one, explain gently why it matters and propose escalating with `escalate_to_care_team` (with the patient's approval). Chest symptoms are an emergency.

## Wound drainage

- Fresh blood or fluid soaking through the dressing after the first day or two.
- Cloudy, yellow, green or smelly discharge at any stage.
- A wound that opens or gapes, or skin over the ankle that looks dark or blistered.
- Could suggest infection or a wound that is not healing.

## Fever

- Temperature above 38 °C, chills or shivering.
- Especially concerning alongside any change at the wound.

## Calf pain (possible blood clot)

- Pain, tenderness, warmth or swelling in the calf of either leg.
- Redness or a firm, cord-like feeling.
- Immobility after surgery raises the risk of deep vein thrombosis. This needs same-day assessment.

## Chest pain or breathlessness

- Sudden shortness of breath, chest pain, coughing up blood, racing heart or fainting.
- Could be a clot that has travelled to the lungs. Call emergency services immediately.

## Hardware pain

- New, sharp or increasing pain directly over the plate or screws, especially when weight bearing.
- A grinding, clicking or shifting sensation in the ankle.
- Metalwork that can be felt pressing on or coming through the skin.
- Could suggest the fixation is irritated, loose, or the bone is not healing as expected.

## Circulation and nerves

- Toes that turn blue, white or cold, or lose feeling.
- Increasing pain that does not ease with elevation and painkillers, or a cast that feels too tight.

## How to respond

- Stay calm and warm. Ask when it started and whether it is getting worse.
- For toe colour, numbness or tightness: advise elevating the leg now and contacting the team.
- For chest symptoms: advise calling emergency services first.
- If the patient agrees, call `escalate_to_care_team` with a short summary of the symptom, when it started, and the current protocol day.
